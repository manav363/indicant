Design proposal v2 · endpoint-driven

The product is bigger than the screen

You were right about v1 — I changed the paint and kept the skeleton. So this time I started by marking every endpoint. That turned up the actual problem: the UI consumes 4 of 24 routes. The six-tier quality gate, the point-in-time universe, corporate-action lineage, the model card and coverage reconciliation are all built and tested, and none of them has ever been drawn. v2 is a new information architecture derived from that map.

The thing worth noticing: the endpoints with no UI are the ones that make this project unlike a stock-tip app. Anyone can show a chart and a BUY badge. Almost nobody can show you why a stock is even allowed into the model, what the gate rejected, and how complete the underlying record is. That evidence is fully built here — and completely invisible.
02 — INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Make the pipeline the interface

v1 (and the current build) is the generic terminal layout: chart left, panel right, table below. Every trading product ships it — which is the tell that it is a default, not a decision. v2 organises the app around the chain the data actually travels, so each stage is a place you can stand, and a prediction can be walked backwards to the government file it came from.

The signature is the chain rail. Not decoration — it is the product's argument as a navigation element. Every node shows its live state, and clicking one opens that stage. It is the one thing here that could not be lifted onto another trading app, because no other trading app can fill it in.

Type also changed, and that matters more than it sounds. v1 used one superfamily (IBM Plex) for every role, which is precisely why it read flat. v2 gives three faces distinct jobs: Archivo — a wide-cut grotesque — for display and the verdict; Inter for everything read; IBM Plex Mono for data. The verdict now sets at 76px in a 112-wide cut against 9px mono labels.

05 — YOUR CALL

What to react to

The substantive question is the chain rail — does making the pipeline the primary navigation feel right, or do you want the app to stay a single stock screen with the other surfaces tucked behind a menu? After that: the verdict at 76px as the lead (v1 had it in a sidebar), the new provenance rail, and whether Archivo as a display face works for you. Palette is unchanged from v1 — it was validated and I did not want to re-open it without a reason.
